How to read this guide

  • Work through Steps 1 to 7 in order the first time. After that, jump straight to the step you need.
  • Orange numbered boxes on a screenshot match the numbered list beside or under it.
  • Tip and Note boxes are short asides. Watch out boxes flag common mistakes.
  • This is the user guide. The technical guide for developers and maintainers is the README on GitHub.
  • The charts in the ten chart types section use the sample tables that come built into the app. The Graph_Maker pictures and the pasted and uploaded workbook pictures come from the real study workbook. The scatter chart and the colour, pattern and warning demonstrations use made-up demonstration numbers, because the workbook's current table has no scatter data or multiple groups. They show how the tool behaves, not study results.

What Figure Studio does

Figure Studio is a web page. You give it a table from the Graph_Maker sheet and it draws a figure sized for a journal column. You then change the style, colours, wording and size, and download the result.

It works out the chart type from the table's column headers, so you never have to say what kind of data it is.

What you need

  • The Hidden Costs study workbook, open in OnlyOffice, Excel or Google Sheets, with a working Graph_Maker sheet.
  • A current desktop browser. The screenshots in this guide were captured in Chrome, so other browsers may look slightly different.
  • An internet connection for the first load, because the page fetches its font from Google Fonts. You also need it if you upload an .xlsx file, which loads a spreadsheet reader. Pasting cells or loading a CSV works without that reader.

The whole job in five moves

  1. Copycells in Graph_Maker
  2. Pastewith Ctrl+V on the page
  3. Adjuststyle, colours, text, size
  4. Checkthe preview
  5. DownloadPNG or SVG
Note. Nothing you load leaves your computer. The page reads and draws everything in your browser.

A tour of the workspace

The window has two parts. The control panel runs down the left. The figure preview sits on the right and redraws every time you change a control.

  1. 1
    Data: where you paste or load a table, and where errors appear.
  2. 2
    Style: the ways this chart type can be drawn. The list changes with the chart type.
  3. 3
    Options: colour palette, one colour or distinct colours, and switches that belong to the chart.
  4. 4
    Text: title, axis labels and footnote, all editable.
  5. 5
    Size: single, 1.5 or double column width, plus a link that clears saved preferences.
  6. 6
    Export bar: Download PNG, SVG and Copy. It stays at the bottom of the panel while the cards scroll.
  7. 7
    Preview: the figure exactly as it will be exported.
  8. 8
    Size readout: the width in inches and the pixel size of the exported PNG.
Tip. When the app opens it already shows an example, so you can try every control before loading your own data.
1

Copy the table from Graph_Maker

Figure Studio does not read the workbook's raw answers. It reads the finished table that the Graph_Maker sheet builds for the chart you choose. Your first job is to set up that chart in the workbook and copy the result.

  1. 1
    The Graph_Maker tab. Click it at the bottom of the window.
  2. 2
    What to plot. The yellow cells are the inputs. Click one and pick from its dropdown list. Bin width takes a whole number. Grey rows (here Item set and Show values as) do not apply to this graph type.
  3. 3
    What the sheet suggests. A chart description, a title, axis labels, a legend text and a footnote, plus a Status line. Wait for the green tick before you copy.
  4. 4
    The chart-ready table. This is what Figure Studio reads. Its column headers tell the app which chart type it is. Here it is selected from the header row (row 18) to the last data row (row 23).
  1. 1a
    Open the Graph_Maker sheet in the study workbook.
  2. 1b
    Choose the Graph type first, then X, Y, group and filter. Check that the Status line shows a green tick and the row and column counts you expect.
  3. 1c
    Click any cell, press Ctrl+A, then Ctrl+C. On a Mac use Cmd. That copies the whole sheet. To copy only the table, select it as in box 4 instead.
Tip. You can copy less. Selecting only the table (box 4), starting at its header row, works just as well. Copying the whole sheet has one advantage: the app also picks up the suggested title, axis labels and footnote from box 3. Step 2 shows the difference.

Load the table

There are four ways to get a table into the app. The first is the one you will use most of the time.

Method A. Paste with Ctrl+V (fastest)

  1. 1
    Click an empty part of the Figure Studio page, away from any text box.
  2. 2
    Press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac).
  3. 3
    The figure redraws at once. The Data card shows a blue tag with the detected chart type, the number of rows, and the word pasted table.
  1. 1
    Detected chart type (Mean by group), row count (5 rows, one per commute category) and where the data came from.
  1. 1
    Title, from the sheet's Suggested title.
  2. 2
    X axis label, from the sheet's X axis.
  3. 3
    Y axis label, from the sheet's Y axis.
  4. 4
    Footnote, from the sheet's Footnote.

Method B. Drop or choose a file

Use this when you have a saved CSV, TSV, text file or the workbook itself. Accepted types are .xlsx, .xlsm, .csv, .tsv and .txt.

  1. 1
    The drop zone. Drag a file anywhere on the page and drop it. While you drag, the whole window dims and says "Drop the workbook to load it".
  2. 2
    The Choose file button opens your computer's file picker.
  3. 3
    The loaded tag. It tells you what is currently on screen.
  4. 4
    How to get the data: opens the three-line recipe from Step 1.
  5. 5
    Paste box and examples: opens the paste box and the example list (Methods C and D).
  1. 1
    The tag shows the chart type and the workbook file name.
  • For a workbook, the app opens the sheet named Graph_Maker and reads its table, as in the figure below. If the table is blank, recalculate and save the workbook in OnlyOffice or Excel first.
  • Reading an .xlsx file needs an internet connection the first time, because the page downloads its spreadsheet reader.

Method C. The paste box

If Ctrl+V on the page does nothing in your browser, paste into a box instead.

  1. 1
    In the Data card, click Paste box and examples to open it.
  2. 2
    Click inside the text area and press Ctrl+V.
  3. 3
    Click Use pasted cells.
  1. 1
    The text area for pasted cells.
  2. 2
    Use pasted cells loads whatever is in the text area.
  3. 3
    The example list: choose a chart type to load a sample table (Method D).

Method D. Try a built-in example

In the same section, the list called Try a table from the workbook loads a sample table for any chart type except scatter. Choose one to see what that chart looks like before you copy real data. The ten chart types near the end of this guide were all made this way.

If something goes wrong

When the app cannot use what you gave it, a red message appears in the Data card and the previous figure stays on screen. Nothing is lost.

  1. 1
    The red message says what is missing. Here it asks for the header row or the whole Graph_Maker sheet. The tag below still shows the earlier, valid table. The most common cause is copying only the data rows and leaving out the header row. Copy again and include the first line.

Undoing your edits

Click Reset text to the sheet's suggestions to discard everything you typed and go back to the automatic text. The title switch stays as it was.

Export the figure

Three buttons sit at the bottom of the control panel. They are always visible, however far you scroll the cards.

  1. 1
    Download PNG: a 300 dpi picture for Word, PowerPoint or a journal upload form.
  2. 2
    SVG: a vector file for editing in Illustrator or Inkscape, or for journals that ask for vector art.
  3. 3
    Copy: puts the PNG on the clipboard so you can paste it straight into a document.

Download PNG

  1. 1
    Check the preview and the size readout.
  2. 2
    Click Download PNG.
  3. 3
    A green message under the buttons confirms the file name. The browser saves it to your Downloads folder.
  1. 1
    The saved file name. It records the chart type, style and size, here figure_meanci_bars_mid.png.

This is the actual file the app produced when the button was clicked for this guide. It measures 1559 by 1078 pixels at 300 dpi.

SVG and Copy

  • Click SVG and you get a file with the same name ending in .svg. Text stays as editable text and shapes stay editable in a vector editor.
  • Click Copy, then paste into Word or PowerPoint with Ctrl+V. The message under the buttons says when the copy is done.
  1. 1
    Copy confirmation.

If the browser blocks saving or copying

Some browsers and embedded views block clipboard access. The app then opens a window showing the finished image. Right-click it and choose Copy image or Save image as. On a phone, press and hold the image. Click Close or press Esc to leave the window.

Tip. Always open the saved PNG once and look at it at full size before you send it anywhere. Check that labels are readable and nothing is cut off.

Quick checklist

Use this once you know your way around.

  1. In Graph_Maker, set the graph type, X, Y, group and filter. Wait for the table.
  2. Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C in the sheet.
  3. Open Figure Studio and press Ctrl+V on the page. Confirm the chart type tag.
  4. Choose a Style.
  5. Set the palette. Use Okabe-Ito or Paul Tol if colour-blind readers matter. Use Greyscale with Patterns for black-and-white print.
  6. Edit the Title (if you want one), axis labels and footnote. Check the sample size in the footnote.
  7. Choose the Size that matches the journal column.
  8. Read the warning line under the preview if there is one.
  9. Click Download PNG, or SVG for vector art, or Copy for a document.
  10. Open the saved file at full size and check it.

Handy facts

  • Ctrl+V works anywhere on the page except inside a text box.
  • Esc closes the image window.
  • Your style, palette, colours, patterns, title switch and size are remembered on this device, even after you close the page. Your data and typed text are not: they reset when you load a new table, and reloading the page starts again from the built-in example.
  • Colours repeat after six groups. If a warning says so, filter the groups in Graph_Maker.

Nothing happens on DownloadAllow downloads for the site. Or use the image window and right-click to save.
The page opens with settings I did not chooseAnother person may have used this browser, or you changed them earlier. Click Reset saved preferences in the Size card.
Labels are tilted or crowdedChoose a wider Size, or a horizontal style such as Horizontal bars or Forest.

A warning about many groups

When a table has more than six groups, a line appears under the preview. Colours repeat after six groups, so groups start to look alike.

  1. 1
    The warning. It says how many groups the table has and suggests filtering in Graph_Maker.
Tip. Filter or merge groups in Graph_Maker rather than relying on readers to tell seven colours apart.
